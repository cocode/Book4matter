# Why Choose Book4matter

Book4matter is open source. We want you to use us, if we are the right choice for you.
If another tool works better for you, that's fine with us. Here are some of the 
popular alternatives, and why you might to use them vs. book4matter.

## Book4matter Advantages

1. It's open source. If you don't like the way something works, you can
change it. 
1. You can use your favorite editor. While many tools
with editors allow you to import, they usually assume you will import once, and
work in their editor from that point on. Why would you want to learn a new editor?
1. Automation. The command line version can be automated. For example, you can have your book automatically 
republised whenever you make a change. Or you can automatically apply formatting changes to multiple books.
1. You can work in text or markdown which work well with version control systems, like git. While these are generally only used by technical folk, being able to track changes is highly useful. And git plays well with automatically publishing a new version of your book, when changes are made.
1. Web app or command line. You can use book4matter without installing anything, or you can run the command line version on your local machine. The choice is yours. 

## Advantages of Other Tools

Many of the alternatives have a broader ranges of predesigned styles for books. Book4matter give you a lot of control, but only one default style. 

Paid tools frequently come with customer support. 

Some have local applications you can run, which can give a nicer interface than a web app.

## Alternative Tools

### Vellum

[Vellum](https://vellum.pub/) is a commercial Mac application supporting 
editing and formatting. It's very easy to use, and has great support and a lot of premade styles. It also makes specific ebooks for each of the major publishing platforms, like 
Amazon, Apple, Google, Kobo, etc., which all have slightly different requirements. 

While you can import, they really expect you will import once, and issues
come up when you want to keep working with your preferred editor.

### Atticus

[Atticus](https://www.atticus.io/) is a web app, like book4matter. It combines editing and formatting across
Windows, Mac, Linux, and Chromebook. It offers manuscript import, ebook and
print output, ready-made templates, a custom theme builder, and
[customer support](https://www.atticus.io/support/).

### Draft2Digital

[Draft2Digital](https://draft2digital.com/faq/) converts uploaded manuscripts
into EPUB and print-ready PDF, with free downloads even if you don't distribute
through the service. It is useful for authors who want straightforward
conversion and optional retailer distribution without adopting a writing
editor. The tradeoffs are reliance on an online service and less direct layout
control than a customizable typesetting pipeline. Distribution is a separate
consideration: commissions and account fees apply under its publishing terms.

### Reedsy

[Reedsy Studio](https://reedsy.com/studio/) combines browser-based writing,
editing, collaboration, and formatting, with manuscript import and EPUB and
print-ready PDF exports. Its core writing and formatting features are free,
making it an accessible option for authors who want a shared workspace.
The tradeoffs are reliance on a hosted application, formatting within its
provided design options, and paid plans for some additional writing and
planning features.

### Pandoc + Typst

Book4matter is just a coordination layer for pandoc and typst, with functions for 
writing books, like indexes, chapters. You could use Pandoc and Typst to do the same. Even if you don't
program, you can have an AI set it up for you. Or just grab the Book4matter source, and 
make the changes you want.
