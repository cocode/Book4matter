Up was the mountain, so Nim climbed it.

The pines gave way to bare rock, and the bare rock gave way to snow. The wind here had teeth. It bit through her wings and rattled the thin bones of her tail, and the higher she went the smaller and greyer she felt, until she was certain there was no fire in her at all — only smoke, and the memory of smoke.

Near the summit she could climb no farther. She curled into a hollow in the rock with the storm howling over her, and she thought: so this is where the smallest dragon stops.
